## The idea in one minute

Keep the whole company in one Genos team, and build each department out of four things that share a name. Search, the AI and @mentions work inside one team, so one team lets everyone find what they are allowed to see. Teams are for separate companies; to work with another company, see "Working with another company: cross-team sharing".

## A department is four things with one name

Take a department called Payments:

- **A project tag "Payments".** Put it on each of the department's projects (open a project, then Edit tags). The Tasks sidebar sorts projects by tag and can filter by it, so a person sees their department's projects together.
- **A mention group "payments".** Settings → Mention groups → New group, then add the people. Writing @payments in a chat message, a task comment or a task body notifies all of them.
- **A team folder "Payments".** In Notes, under Team Notes, make a folder and choose who can open it: everyone, or only people you add. You can add a whole mention group at once. This is where the department's knowledge lives: runbooks, policies, meeting notes. Folders nest, so a sub-team gets a subfolder.
- **A public group chat "payments · general".** Use New Group Message and choose Public Group. Anyone in the team can find it and join.

A sub-team repeats the same four things with a longer name: @payments-billing, a "Billing" subfolder, "payments · billing", and the tag on its projects.

## Keeping something inside a smaller group

Use the private version. What each one does today:

- **A private group chat**: only people you add, or whose request to join you approve, are in it.
- **A private project**: only its members open it. Anyone else can ask to join.
- **A private team folder**: it opens only for the people you add, and it does not appear in anyone else's folder list.

The name of a private project still shows to everyone in the team (in Join Project, and in Spotlight when someone types #). The name of a private group chat shows in the chat search box, with a lock. To hide the name as well, tick "Hidden: only members can see it exists" when you create the project or group chat. Only a private project or chat can be hidden. Then non-members cannot find it or ask to join; members must add people themselves.

## Where does it go?

- Talk about one piece of work: that project's chat.
- A decision or spec for one task: the task's note, so it stays with the work.
- What the whole department should know: a note in its team folder.
- A meeting recap: a note in the department's folder, or on the chat.
- A topic with no project: a public group chat named "department · topic". Leave it when it is over.
- Your own drafts and your day: My Notes and To-Do.

Name chats "department · topic". Then searching for the department's name lists all of them together.

## Join little, find everything

- Join your own sub-team's projects and chats, plus two or three others. You do not need to join something to find it.
- Press Cmd-K (Ctrl-K on Windows) for Spotlight. Type # to pick a project, task, note or group chat, and @ for people and mention groups. Public projects you have not joined show up there too.
- In the Tasks sidebar, Join Project lists the team's projects you have not joined.
- In the chat sidebar menu, "Browse group chats" lists the team's public group chats, with a search box and a Join button. Typing a group's name in the chat search box also finds it.

## Keeping your own sidebar short

- Pin the few chats you open all day (Pin Chat). The rest sort by latest message.
- Mute a noisy chat with the bell in its header.
- Tag group chats for yourself (the ⋮ menu on a chat, Tags). The tags are private to you and filter your list.
- Star the milestones, notes and folders you return to (Favorites), and save the task filters you keep rebuilding (Saved Filters).
- Hide the parts of Genos you do not use: Settings → General → Sidebar.

## Tidying up

A project or a group chat you no longer use can be archived instead of deleted.

## A ready-made example

If you kept "Include sample instruction data" ticked when you created the team, you already have one: the tag "Sample dept", @sample-dept, the "Sample dept" folders, two projects and two group chats. Open the note "How the sample dept keeps its work tidy" in Team Notes, copy what is useful, and delete the rest. To remove all of the sample data at once, the team owner opens Team Profile and chooses "Remove sample data…".
